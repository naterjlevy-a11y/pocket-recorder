The one thing that cannot be settled on paper is whether Bluetooth escapes a metal tube, and that costs about $60 to test.

What can kill this

Ranked by how likely they are to end the project, not by how hard they are to fix.

unproven Bluetooth may not escape the metal

A sealed metal tube attenuates 2.4 GHz by roughly 6 dB per millimetre. The design puts a chip antenna in a 5 mm non-metal tip with the 35 mm steel body acting as its counterpoise — which is sound theory, and 35 mm is a good length for it. But it is unproven in this exact geometry and no calculation settles it.

Test: feed a split tube from an nRF52840 module with a u.FL connector over thin coax and measure signal strength across a room. $60, one evening. If it fails, the product becomes plastic or ceramic and the whole look changes.

likely Talk time could be 1.6 hours, not 5

Everything depends on how much current the firmware actually draws while recording. My estimate is 4–6 mA. Shipping competitors — Plaud, Omi — draw 11–16 mA. At 13 mA this device gives 1.6 hours per charge rather than 5.2.

Test: measure it on the dev board in Phase 1, before committing to a cell size. Still a usable product at 1.6 h — but know the number before you promise it.

open The custom battery may not be quotable at low volume

The stepped cell is the only true custom part. Shaped-cell suppliers quote samples readily but production minimums can be thousands of units. If the minimum is unaffordable, the fallback is a plain flat cell, which costs about 6 mAh — roughly 45 minutes of talking.

Do today: email Grepow. It costs nothing and it is the longest lead item in the project. Ask specifically whether the protection board can fold beside the cell rather than in line with it — that would win back 4 mm of length.

open The 0.3 mm ball-pitch chip is hard to manufacture

The nRF54L15 in its smallest package needs a 6-layer HDI board with laser-drilled vias, and machine assembly. No hand soldering, no cheap prototype board service.

Mitigation: JLCPCB fabricates and assembles this class of board affordably. Expect roughly $600 for a first run of five, not the $200 a simple board would cost.

watch Chain noise into the microphone

A metal bar on a metal chain behaves as a contact microphone. Every link tick can be louder than your voice.

Mitigation: suspend the mic in a silicone boot so it never touches the shell, and offer a leather cord — quieter, and a second look rather than a compromise.
